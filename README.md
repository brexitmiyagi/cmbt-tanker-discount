# cmbt-tanker-discount

Data and code behind my Seeking Alpha piece on CMB.TECH (CMBT), November 2026. The question it ends up answering: what does CMB.TECH's discount to NAV actually assume? Broker values are what ships sell for, and ships sell close to what a new one costs to build. earnings_nav.py marks every ship on the water on what it can earn instead. On the 2015-2025 through-cycle rates the stock trades at 0.90 times NAV; on the rates a newbuild needs to pay for itself (replacement_cost.py) it's 0.58 times, against 0.57 on broker values. So the discount is the market splitting the difference.

The same question runs through earnings. gain_decomposition.py splits every tanker sale since 2023 into the part from the books depreciating faster than the market (which would recur) and the part from ship prices rising (which recurs only if prices keep rising). The price part is 82% to 126% of the gains across age curves and lightweight estimates. With sale gains out, the two businesses CMB.TECH is made of earned 6.8% on equity; with them in, 7.9%; the price needs 7.2%. forward_gains.py scales sale gains to the yearly drift in ship prices instead of switching them on or off.

I use a drift of -2% a year. earnings_implied_value.py gives -2.3% to -4.5% on through-cycle rates, but replacement_cost.py shows a Capesize newbuild needs $25,799 a day against $27,273 for today's five-year-old price, so that gap is mostly history against replacement cost, and on that test prices only fall if newbuild prices do. After the last orderbook peak they fell 7.6% a year (orderbook.py), and the VLCC orderbook is 33.1% of the fleet, with net growth of 19% to 39% in 2027-29 after scrapping (supply_net.py). backtest_eiv.py checks the earnings test at Feb 2016 and Feb 2023: right direction 3 of 4, with hindsight through-cycle rates.

With the bear case at Kalshi's 33%, I rate it a Sell, target $15.05, low conviction: -0.8% expected total return against a 3.2% Sell line. The Sell holds for any drift below +0.5% a year, for a bear weight above 12.5%, and at a cost of equity from 7.5% to 9.4%. There's an Excel version of the earnings model in results/CMBT_model.xlsx.

Earlier drafts had a Buy at $18 to $21 and Sells at $17, $16.50, $15.65 and $15.40 on different assumptions, and some script descriptions below still quote those earlier targets. The published numbers are the v17 block at the end of rating.py. rating.py replaces the target and rating in yield_check.py and payoff_both_routes.py, which are kept because they're part of how I got here. Those workings are still in here (payoff.py, holdco_discount.py, valuation.py, target_bridge.py) because they're part of how I got here.

## How to run it

You need Python 3. No packages beyond the standard library.

    python scripts/tieout.py

That runs every script, writes the output into results/, and fails if any number quoted in the article stops reproducing.

## What each script does

- nav_bridge.py: book equity per share, then NAV two ways. Route 1 backs the ship value uplift out of the 58.1% value-adjusted equity ratio on slide 5 ($25.02 a share). Route 2 uses the $12.2bn broker fleet value on slide 4 against vessels at book ($30.67). Then it lines the gap to the market cap up against the tanker fleet, and runs the markdown scenarios ($17.24, $21.13, $20.18, $12.41).
- fcf_stress.py: takes the 2027 free cash flow by division from slide 8, works out what each $1,000 a day is worth per division and the implied cash breakevens, and reruns 2027 at Q3 2025 rates ($722m on the slide, $490m with tankers at Q3 2025 rates, $158m with everything at Q3 2025 rates).
- q4_floor.py: a floor for Q4 2026 from fixtures already booked ($635.5m, $2.19 a share).
- tanker_sales.py: the 2026 tanker sales and gains, split before and after the strait shut ($677.7m total, $431.8m on VLCCs).
- who_it_trades_with.py: weekly returns of CMBT against Frontline, DHT, Star Bulk and Genco.
- peer_discount.py: the cross-check I should have run first. Star Bulk trades at 0.76 times its own fleet value less net debt. Put CMB.TECH's non-tanker NAV on that same discount and the market is left paying $1,945m for the tankers, 62% of what the brokers say they're worth. At Star Bulk's discount the whole company is worth $19.04 to $23.35 a share.
- target_bridge.py: the $21 target, the covenant headroom (ship values can fall about 41% before the 30% value-adjusted equity covenant binds), what Q4 adds to NAV after the Q3 payout (+$0.45), and the Belgian withholding on the $0.65.
- tc_drag.py: the part I missed in the first two versions. 12 of CMB.TECH's tankers are on time charter (slide 11), and the Suezmax time charter average has stayed between $31,328 and $34,726 a day from Q1 2025 to Q3 2026, war included. At Q3 spot the 717 charter days gave up $82.9m; at the Q4-to-date spot rate the same days would give up $246.8m. Slide 8 implies about 6.2 Suezmaxes still on charter through 2027, costing $108.2m against the company's own deck. Marking the tankers for the charters takes $0.63 to $0.98 a share off NAV.
- fleet_table.py: the crude tanker fleet ship by ship (data/fleet_crude_tankers.csv). 10 of the 14 Suezmaxes were on time charter in April, six of them Valero ships, and 7 of the 8 tankers sold since were trading spot or in the pool.
- model.py: my quarterly model, Q4 2026 to Q4 2028. Profit by segment is days x (rate - the company's own P&L breakeven), spot days from slide 8, charter days at the Q3 charter averages, and one calibrated line for everything else ($12.7m a quarter). Cash, distributions, net debt and book equity roll forward. It prints EPS against consensus, the debt path, the cost of debt and the debt service schedule from the 20-F.
- valuation.py: peer price/NAV from each peer's own numbers (Star Bulk 0.76x, Genco 0.79x, DHT 1.17x, INSW 1.09x), CMB.TECH's own price/NAV history (0.61x to 0.76x), the sum of the parts ($19.88 to $20.27 on the lower NAV), forward NAV ($26.14 at end-2027) and the sensitivity grid.
- fleet_valuation.py: my own check on the fleet value, ship by ship, from public broker age curves (Compass Maritime 16 Nov 2026, Clarksons 15-year points). Dry bulk comes out at $6,915m against the company's $7,017m. It also marks each chartered ship ($458m to $600m) and gives a NAV of $28.71 to $29.20 a share.
- hormuz_paths.py: four paths for the strait on one basis (broker-value forward NAV, path-specific charter marks, 0.62x), the bear case on the same basis, multiple sensitivity, the Polymarket and Kalshi cross-checks, the reverse check and the sensitivity grid.
- nav_reconcile.py: the two company NAVs (from the value-adjusted ratio and from broker values) side by side at three dates, and how much of the gap the obvious explanations can cover.
- structure_and_green.py: ownership and float, short interest, and what an ammonia Newcastlemax needs to earn 8%.
- build_excel.py: writes results/CMBT_model.xlsx, the same model as live formulas. Blue cells are inputs. excel_check.py checks the Excel values against the Python.
- payoff.py: the upside case only, route 2 held at its best multiple (0.62x): $18.00.
- holdco_discount.py: the test the article turns on. Peers on enterprise value over broker fleet value (31 Dec 2025 and now), CMB.TECH's segments on those multiples less charter marks, and the extra discount the share price implies on both NAVs. Route 1: -3% before the war, 8% now, tankers implied at 0.92x against peers at 1.12x. Route 2: 45% before, 29% now. It also prints what the share is worth if the pre-war discount came back ($18.23 to $19.67 on route 1, $13.18 to $13.52 on route 2).
- payoff_both_routes.py: the target and the real bear case. End-2027 NAV across the four Hormuz paths, weighted on Kalshi's 25 Nov prices, on each route at CMB.TECH's lowest, current and best price/NAV. Target $17.00. Bear (reopens in Q1, dry bulk -20%, lowest multiple) $10.72 on route 1 and $9.29 on route 2. Distributions $2.35, or $1.70 if bought after the 9 Dec ex-date.
- through_cycle.py: what a normal year looks like. Euronav/CMB.TECH realised spot and charter averages 2015-2025 (data/euronav_realised_rates.csv), the Clarksons-implied 10-year Capesize average, the full-cycle charter book, and return on equity for Euronav, Star Bulk and Golden Ocean from SEC XBRL company facts (data/roe_history.csv).
- dry_bulk_cases.py: what 2028 looks like, the assumption the call turns on. Base: 2027 at the company deck, 2028 halfway to the through-cycle average (EPS $1.04). Straight to through-cycle: -$0.50 (today's cost base loses money there, which is why the normal year is valued on returns). 2025 rates: $0.48. Forward price held: $2.57. model.py takes CAPE27, CAPE28 and TANK28 (phased, cycle, 2025, deck).
- holdco_series.py: the discount as a series, every company priced on the date of its own fleet values, with error bands from the rounding in peer ratios. Route 1: 1%, 28%, 15% (Dec, Jun, Sep). Route 2: 46%, 42%, 32%. Also the same-date anchors ($17.62 route 1, $15.46 route 2 on today's price).
- yield_check.py: the cash check and the rating. Q4 2026-2028 free cash flow, then a normal year for ever as a return on end-2028 book. The price needs 7.2% at an 8.2% cost of equity (6.0% at 7%, 9.1% at 10%). DCF at the predecessors' 6.8%: $16.89; at the three-company average 7.7%: $18.39; at Star Bulk's 9.7%: $21.43. 50/50 with the asset view: +2.5% expected return, target $16.77 rounded down to $16.50. BUY needs about 9.0%.
- payoff_both_routes.py also prints the bear weight where the rating flips (18.9% with bull at 25%).
- roe_windows.py: every 8-year window of return on equity the filings allow, reported and adjusted (impairments added back, disposal gains out), plus the common 2015-2024 window. data/roe_history.csv comes from SEC XBRL company facts.
- hormuz_multiple.py: CMB.TECH's weekly price against Kalshi's odds of a reopening before 2027 (data/kalshi_hormuz_daily.csv, from Kalshi's public API). Correlation -0.83.
- nav_covenant.py: the loan definition of the value-adjusted ratio (fully owned vessels in pre-agreed segments only) and the size of the gap it explains.
- ammonia_premium.py: what the ammonia Newcastlemaxes need: a charter premium of about $7,659/day at 2025 rates to earn 8%.
- war_money_screen.py: who kept the war money in Q3 2026, seven owners, about $312m handed to charterers. CMB.TECH $88.8m (32% of Q3 profit), Frontline $77.7m, DHT $54.5m, Hafnia $52.1m, Scorpio $40.6m, Teekay $20.7m; International Seaways gained $22.0m through profit-share VLCC charters. Rows marked derived back the charter days out of blended rates or vessel counts (method in data/war_money_q3_2026.csv).
- pair_trade.py: short CMBT / long DHT. Hedge ratio 0.64, pair volatility 31%, carry -1.2% a year before borrow (+2.5% after the 9 Dec ex-date).
- rating.py: the rating, done so the target and rating can't disagree. Both methods give a 12-month price (the asset view probability-weighted, $15.34; the DCF value grown a year less distributions), and the expected return is their average plus $2.35 of distributions. Grid of normal-year return (6.8%, 7.9%, 8.9%, 9.9%) against cost of equity (7.5%, 8.2%, 9.4%). Published case: target $15.63, rounded up to $15.65 against the call, +2.6%. The SELL needs the normal year below 6.9%, and holds while the bear case carries more than 21.6%.
- vessel_sales.py: every ship sold 2023 to June 2026, 58 of them, with sale price, book value and gain from the company's own disposal notes (data/vessel_sales_2023_2026.csv). $1,594m of gains, 44% of it from the 24 VLCCs sold to Frontline for $2,347m, priced on an average of three broker valuations. Seven VLCCs sold just before the war went for $651m and are worth about $1,223m at today's broker values for the same ages.
- hormuz_sensitivity.py: the war-in-the-price test redone on changes. Daily correlation of CMBT with changes in Kalshi's reopening odds is -0.06 (levels gave -0.83), and on the ten biggest odds days CMBT went the expected way five times. Daily prices in data/daily_closes_nasdaq.csv.
- beta_check.py: beta against SPY before and during the war, daily and weekly. CMBT 0.52 daily and 0.89 weekly over 18 months; pre-war weekly peer betas run from -1.00 to 1.28.
- covenant_timeline.py: the bridge facility (the only agreement on file, with the narrow vessel definition) still had $122.5m drawn at 31 Dec 2025 and nil at 30 Jun 2026, and the $2bn facility was repriced in the same half.
- gain_decomposition.py: the test the call turns on. For 50 tankers sold 2023 to June 2026, cost backed out of book with the 20-year policy (residual = lightweight x $460/ldt; lightweight 42,000 t for a VLCC and 22,000 t for a Suezmax are my estimates), then the flat-price value at the sale age from Signal Ocean's 10-year/5-year VLCC value ratio (0.69 in 2016, 0.755 in 2023, 0.80 high case). Depreciation part -$364m to +$130m, price part $1,403m to $1,897m, of $1,533m of gains. The 2023-24 Suezmaxes sold in 2026 are estimated from their $66.4m build cost. Build years in data/vessel_sales_built.csv.
- newbuild_share.py: every VLCC kept is a 2025-27 delivery, 38 of 112 bulkers were built 2023-26, $572m under construction and $843m of capex to come, against a $132m VLCC newbuild price today.
- spike_history.py: price to book for INSW (quarterly), Euronav and DHT (annual) around the 2019-20 tanker spikes, from SEC XBRL and monthly closes: falls of 38% to 56%, against the 35% fall in CMB.TECH's price/NAV that the bear case assumes.
- war_money_v2.py: the Q3 charter giveaway redone once I found CMB.TECH books Suezmax profit shares in its spot line (Q3 release, footnote 3 to the spot rate), while Frontline, DHT and INSW book theirs in the time charter line (data/profit_share_booking_q3_2026.csv). Two methods, peer Suezmax spot ($103,813 average) and a 50/50 split; range $16m to $81m, central $53.5m, against $88.8m before.
- hormuz_paths.py: charter marks now on the actual book (data/suezmax_charter_book_ir_2026-11-26.csv, from CMB.TECH investor relations, cited with their consent), options assumed exercised, profit-split ships at half the gap. Set CHARTERS=assumed for the old 3-year version.
- forward_gains.py: sale gains scaled to ship-price drift, 0.31 points of return on equity per point of yearly drift, calibrated on 2016-23. Expected 12-month return from +9.4% (prices keep rising) to -7.3% (back to Feb 2023 levels over ten years).
- ammonia_fuel.py: extra fuel cost a day at sea for a Newcastlemax on ammonia against VLSFO, at 60% and 85% load. CMB.TECH pays the fuel on the Fortescue charter, per investor relations. Prices in data/fuel_prices_2026-11.csv.
- earnings_implied_value.py: five-year-old VLCC, Suezmax and Capesize valued on one year at today's one-year charter, a year halfway down, then through-cycle rates to scrap at 25, at 8%. Prints the gap to price, the day rate the price needs, and the drift that closes the gap over 10 or 20 years.
- orderbook.py: orderbook against the fleet (CMB.TECH Q3 2026 deck, as reported by Investing.com; check the deck itself), VLCC capacity growth to 2029, and the 2008 episode (Euronav 2015 prospectus, Poten).
- sector_screen.py: sale gains as a share of profit in the upcycle, gains less impairments as a share of equity in falling markets, and NAV sensitivity to ship values, for Euronav, International Seaways, Golden Ocean, Star Bulk, Frontline and DHT where XBRL allows. data/peer_gains_xbrl.csv.
- short_checks.py: FINRA short interest and days to cover (data/short_interest_2026-11.csv). No free borrow rate.
- rating.py (v17 block): asset view carries the same drift as the cash view; bear weight 33% (Kalshi) with 25% as the sensitivity.
- replacement_cost.py: the day rate a newbuild needs over 25 years at 8% (VLCC $132m, Suezmax $90m, Capesize $75.2m; data/newbuild_prices_2026.csv). The Capesize answer, $25,799, is close to the $27,273 today's five-year-old price needs, so the earnings test mostly measures the gap between history and replacement cost.
- backtest_eiv.py: the earnings test at Feb 2016 (Capesize) and Feb 2023 (VLCC, Suezmax, Capesize) from broker weekly reports (data/backtest_points.csv). Right direction 3 of 4; the miss is Capesize 2023. Through-cycle rates are hindsight.
- earnings_nav.py: all ships on the water marked at their own ages on earnings, on through-cycle and on replacement-cost long-run rates. Price/NAV 0.90 and 0.58 against 0.57 on broker values.
- supply_net.py: VLCC net fleet growth 2027-29 under three scrapping cases and the shadow-fleet cases (data/vlcc_supply_2026.csv).
- frontline_side.py: the 24 VLCCs from Frontline's side: paid $2,347m, worth about $4,704m on today's curve.
- tieout.py: the check.

## Things to be careful with

- Route 1 assumes the 58.1% ratio is value-adjusted equity over value-adjusted total assets on the 30 Sep balance sheet. The company doesn't publish the exact definition. Route 2 gives a higher number, and I use the lower one.
- The 72.1% decade-average markdown is for a 15-year-old VLCC. CMB.TECH's tankers average 7.4 years, so applying it to the whole fleet is harsh on purpose.
- The FCF slopes are straight lines through the company's base and +10% decks. Real cash flow isn't linear that far out, especially near breakeven.
- The Q4 floor uses a spot split of 2 VLCCs and 6 Suezmaxes that I inferred from slide 11 and the Q3 time charter days. It ignores ships sold but still trading until delivery.
- The 10-year average for Capesizes is read off the company's chart, not a published number, so I only describe it as roughly double.
- My gains tally is $677.7m. The company's slide says 9 VLCCs for $431.8m and 5 Suezmaxes for $246.2m. The small Suezmax gap is Sienna's gain moving after delivery.

- Star Bulk's NAV here is fleet value less net debt and nothing else, and the fleet value is 30 Sep while the net debt is 18 Nov. It's rough. It's the same rough on both sides, which is the point.
- The covenant math assumes the bank definition of value-adjusted equity matches the 58.1% on slide 5. The 20-F gives the thresholds (27.5% in 2026, 30% after) but the loan agreements may define it differently.

- The charter markdown is mine. The company doesn't publish charter end dates or rates per ship. I use the fleet average rate, slide 8's implied spot days for 2027, the pre-war Q3 2025 spot rate after that, 8%, and 3 or 8 more years. Profit splits exist on some charters (Cap Grace and Cap Joseph at least) and aren't visible in the average, so the true cost could be lower.

- nav_bridge.py still prints the decade-average markdown scenarios from my first workings. The final article doesn't use them: that benchmark is a 15-year-old VLCC, and CMB.TECH's VLCCs are under a year old on average.

- Genco's and DHT's fleet values are backed out of their own leverage ratios (net loan-to-value 18%, financial leverage 10.5%). Genco's ratio is rounded, and I'm assuming DHT's is net debt over fleet value. INSW's is management's "nearly $5 billion" on the call; I use $4.9bn. Safe Bulkers was left out because its leverage ratio could be gross or net and the two give 0.5x or 1.4x.
- The model holds 'everything else' flat at the Q3 level, ignores interest savings from paying down debt, and uses the 2026 budget breakevens for 2027 and 2028. 2028 tanker rates go back to Q3 2025 levels; that's my assumption, not the company's.
- CMB.TECH's price/NAV history is only four points (Q4 2025, Q2 2026, Q3 2026 and today), because I could only get the value-adjusted equity ratio from those presentations.

- The Newcastlemax and Panamax curves are scaled from Capesize and Kamsarmax by deadweight. That's my assumption; no public table I could find covers them.
- The Hormuz probabilities (20/25/25/30) are my judgement. The Polymarket and Kalshi cross-checks map 'traffic back to normal by end-Q1 2027' to path A, 'by end-2027' to A+B, and the rest to D. Some of those contracts are thin.
- fleet_valuation.py takes a curve file as an argument: run it with secondhand_values_kobc_2026-10-01.csv for the KOBC check (dry bulk 8.7% below the company).
- valuation.py, tc_drag.py and the route-1 numbers in nav_bridge.py are earlier workings on the 58.1%-ratio basis; the target now uses the broker basis in hormuz_paths.py.
- The secondhand values and 12-month charter rates are from one broker report (Compass, 16 Nov 2026). Clarksons via Energy News Beat has VLCC 5- and 10-year values $5m-10m higher.

- Caveats that are in this README rather than the article: Genco's and DHT's NAVs are backed out of rounded leverage ratios and INSW's uses management's 'nearly $5 billion'; the Newcastlemax and Panamax value curves are scaled from Capesize and Kamsarmax; the two FSOs aren't covered by any public curve; the Q4 floor uses an inferred spot split (3 VLCCs, 6 Suezmaxes); war-risk premium figures are from July 2026; the Polymarket 2027 contracts are thin; short interest is the latest Seeking Alpha figure, date not shown; the Q1 2026 release describes the 5-year Suezmax charter ship as 'Cedar (2011, 165,000 dwt)' while the 20-F lists Cedar as a 2022 ship.

## Gaps I couldn't fill with free data

There's no free price/NAV history for tanker stocks, so spike_history.py uses price to book. Some sold ships were bought second-hand, so their cost in gain_decomposition.py is implied from the policy rather than the actual purchase price. A 10-15 year regression of Capesize rates on fleet and demand growth: the annual series sit behind Baltic Exchange and Clarksons paywalls. Instead I use the companies' own realised averages for tankers and a Clarksons-implied 10-year Capesize average (2025 Baltic average / 1.4, approximate). DHT's XBRL net income for 2015-2018 has signs I couldn't reconcile, so DHT isn't in the return history. Golden Ocean's history before 2015 is the Knightsbridge Tankers filer. Some impairments aren't tagged in XBRL (Euronav 2021, Golden Ocean 2019-2020), so those years can't be adjusted. Star Bulk's 2010-2013 equity was very small, which makes its early windows extreme. I'd said CMB.TECH discloses only sale gains. Its 20-F and half-year report do list sale prices and book values ship by ship (vessel_sales.py), but not for the Q3 2026 sales yet, and I can't get broker values per ship at each sale date for free, so the at-market test uses the Frontline pricing basis and Signal Ocean's age points. The ammonia charter rates aren't disclosed by CMB.TECH or by Fortescue (FY26 results, climate transition plan, June 2026 announcement). Investor relations told me the Fortescue contract is index-linked consecutive-voyage with fuel for CMB.TECH's account, but not the index or the rate. I don't have paid broker valuations for single ships or a price/NAV history for 2019-21. The INSW profit-share formula isn't disclosed. The newer CMB.TECH facility agreements aren't filed, so the covenant definition after the bridge is open. TORM and Ardmore don't split time charter days by class, so they're not in the screen. 
CMB.TECH's Q1 2026 release doesn't carry the fleet value split or the value-adjusted ratio, so the series skips March for CMB.TECH. No tanker peer gave a fleet value or leverage ratio for 30 June 2026 that I could find, so June brackets tankers between December and September. Star Bulk gave a fleet value at 31 March and 30 September only. Frontline, Teekay and Okeanis don't publish fleet values. INSW's net LTV is so low (about 6%) that backing a fleet value out of it is too noisy to use. There's no public calendar-2027 Capesize FFA quote I could verify. Borrow cost for a CMBT short isn't public.
